# Inheritance tax on pensions from 6 April 2027 and self-employed pension facts (E33 S7)

Read 2026-11-25 by an independent Fable decider (signer `fable-decider`) from primary sources. Facts marked [U] were not confirmed in a primary source that day. Not legal advice.

## Sources

- FA: Finance Act 2026 (c. 11), <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11>, ss.66 to 72.
- TN1: HMRC, Inheritance Tax on pensions technical note, updated 29 May 2026, <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inheritance-tax-on-pensions-technical-note/technical-note-inheritance-tax-on-pensions>.
- TN2: HMRC, Technical note 2, 27 August 2026, <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inheritance-tax-on-pensions-technical-note-2/technical-note-2-further-information-on-inheritance-tax-and-pensions>.

## 1. Status: enacted

- Finance Act 2026 ss.66 to 71, Royal Assent 18 March 2026. s.71: applies to deaths on or after 6 April 2027; no commencement regulations needed.
- Information regulations SI 2026/818 laid 15 July 2026; further consequential SIs expected before April 2027 (TN2 2.1).
- Budget 28 October 2026 (Finance Bill 2026-27, not yet enacted): payment notices by prospective personal representatives; a choice of component for the 36% test. No change to scope or date.

## 2. Scope

- New IHTA 1984 s.150A: the member is treated as beneficially entitled, immediately before death, to "notional pension property" in registered schemes, QNUPS and s.615(3) schemes (FA s.66). Money purchase: all pot property usable for death benefits, crystallised or not (TN2 example 1). Defined benefit: lump sum death benefits and certain continuation payments (TN1 3.2.2).
- Excluded (s.150A(6)): dependants' scheme pensions and their trivial commutation; dependants' or nominees' annuities from a joint life annuity; death in service benefits (current employment only; a deferred member's lump sum is in scope, TN2 4.3).
- Spouse or civil partner and charity exemptions apply (FA s.69; IHTA ss.18, 23).

## 3. Who reports and pays

- Personal representatives report and are liable (new IHTA s.210, FA s.67). They can tell the scheme to pay the tax on the pension when it is at least £1,000, paid within 35 days (s.226B). Schemes may withhold up to 50% of a beneficiary's entitlement for up to 15 months (s.226A).
- Tax due by the end of the sixth month after death; interest after (TN1 2.3).

## 4. Thresholds and rates

- Nil-rate band £325,000, residence nil-rate band £175,000, taper from £2,000,000 (£1 lost per £2), frozen to 5 April 2031 (FA s.72 amending FA 2021 s.86). Rate 40%, 36% where 10% or more of the net estate goes to charity. Unused bands transfer to a surviving spouse or civil partner (<https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax>).
- Pension funds count towards the £2m taper: IHTA s.8D(5)(d) takes the estate immediately before death, and s.150A puts the pension in it at that moment. Statutory inference; HMRC does not say so expressly [U].
- Tax on the whole estate is apportioned between the pension and the rest pro rata (TN2 5.2); exact HMRC formula and tool not yet published [U].

## 5. Income tax on inherited pensions

- Death before 75: tax-free, lump sums subject to the lump sum and death benefit allowance £1,073,100. Death at 75 or over: taxed as the beneficiary's income (<https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-pension-death-benefits>).
- New ITEPA s.567B (FA s.70): inheritance tax paid on the pension is deducted from the taxable pension income (the lesser of the two). Whether a claim is needed is not stated [U].

## 6. Self-employed pension facts, 2026/27

- Relief at source: the provider adds 20%; higher and additional rate relief (a further 20% or 25%) is claimed through Self Assessment; Scottish rates differ (<https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-your-private-pension/pension-tax-relief>).
- Relief on contributions up to 100% of relevant UK earnings, or £3,600 gross where earnings are lower (FA 2004 s.190; PTM044100). Annual allowance £60,000, tapered above £260,000 adjusted income to a floor of £10,000 (PTM057100).
- Pension contributions do not reduce Class 4 NI (NIM24605). Class 2 is treated as paid at profits of £7,105 or more; voluntary £3.65 a week below (<https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates>). At least 10 qualifying years for any new State Pension.
