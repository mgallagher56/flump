> ## Owner to review against s.414 before merge
>
> The Scottish Gift Aid change below is an owner ruling implemented from the statute text only. No HMRC guidance page for Scottish taxpayers was read, so every affected vector is UNVERIFIED. Please read s.414 yourself before merging.
>
> **Statute (ITA 2007 s.414, "Relief for gifts to charity", read on https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/3/section/414 and its 06/04/2020 and 06/04/2025 point-in-time versions, which have the same wording):**
>
> 1. "An individual who makes a gift to a charity which is a qualifying donation is entitled to the relief set out in subsection (2)."
> 2. "The Income Tax Acts have effect in their application to the individual for the tax year in which the gift is made as if-- (a) the gift had been made after deduction of income tax at the basic rate, and (b) the basic rate limit and the higher rate limit (see section 10) and additionally, in the case of a Scottish taxpayer, the upper limit for the Scottish basic rate and the limits for any Scottish rates above the Scottish basic rate, were increased by an amount equal to the grossed up amount of the gift."
> 3. "See subsection (7) of section 535 of ITTOIA 2005 (gains from contracts for life insurance etc : top slicing relief) for provision about how relief under this Chapter is to be ignored for the purpose of calculating relief under that section."
>
> There is no subsection (4). Subsection (3) is only a cross-reference and does not alter the Scottish rule. The Scottish words in (2)(b) were inserted by SI 2017/468 reg.10(2) and substituted from 6 April 2018 by SI 2018/459 art.6(4)(b), both shown on legislation.gov.uk.
>
> **Rule implemented** (`calculateIncomeTaxOnTaxable` in `uk-tax-year.ts`, used by `calculateUkTax`): for a Scottish taxpayer, grossed-up gift = net x 1.25; the upper limit of the Basic band (the Scottish basic rate, 20%) and every limit (lower and upper) of each band above it (Intermediate, Higher, Advanced where the year has it, Top) rise by that amount. Gift Aid also still reduces adjusted net income for the personal allowance taper. England, Wales and Northern Ireland are unchanged.
>
> **Ambiguity note:** the Starter band (19%) sits below the Scottish basic rate and is not named in (2)(b), so it is kept unshifted: its upper limit stays put. The Intermediate band (21%) sits above the Scottish basic rate, so by the text its limits shift (its lower limit is the Basic upper limit). The text itself does not define the band names: ITA 2007 s.6A (which defined the Scottish basic, higher and additional rates) was omitted for 2017/18 onward, and s.11B is the Welsh section, so the reviewer's pointer to s.11B does not help. "Scottish basic rate" is taken as the band the Scottish Parliament and the engine table (`UK_SCOTLAND_INCOME_TAX`) call "Basic" (20%). If you read "Scottish basic rate" differently, or think the Starter band should move with it, tell me and the one rule in `calculateIncomeTaxOnTaxable` changes.
>
> **Further s.414 caveats (read before ruling):**
>
> - "Scottish basic rate" is an INFERENCE: the statute does not define Scottish band names. s.414(2)(b) says "...the upper limit for the Scottish basic rate and the limits for any Scottish rates above the Scottish basic rate ... were increased by an amount equal to the grossed up amount of the gift". The engine takes the Basic band, found by its 20% rate (not its display name), as the Scottish basic rate. s.11B is the Welsh rates section, not a Scottish definition.
> - `calculateUkTax` still folds savings interest into the Scottish non-savings bands and ignores the basic and higher rate LIMIT increase for savings and dividends (a simplification): the gift widens only the non-savings Scottish bands, and savings and dividend tax are computed as without a gift.
> - `analyzeTaxTrap` for Scotland does not shift bands for a gift, so the displayed marginal rate can differ from the band actually reached by a taxpayer who has made a gift.
>

(b) `calculateUKEWNITakeHome` still rounds tax, NI, pension and student loan to whole pounds (up to about 1 pound difference versus `calculateUkTax`). D5-a is not fully applied to that wrapper.

(c) Silent unknown-year fallbacks remain: `calculateUKTax` falls back to 2024/25, `calculateUKEWNITakeHome` falls back similarly, and uk-tax.ts:80 (`calculateStudentLoanDeduction` default thresholds, `UK_STUDENT_LOANS[getCurrentUKTaxYear()] ?? 2026/27`) falls back to 2026/27. See (l) for the 6 April 2027 consequence. They were deliberately not changed to throw, to avoid breaking app callers outside this track. Also (task I6): the no-argument trap entry points (`analyzeTaxTrap`, `calculate100kTaxTrap`, `calculateTaxTrapRelief`) default to the latest supported year (2026/27) once the clock passes the last supported year, instead of throwing, so the UI callers do not crash; an explicitly passed unsupported year still throws.

(d) The PSA logic is still year-blind (hardcoded 50,270 / 125,140 Scottish and PSA thresholds). RESOLVED (task I6) for `analyzeTaxTrap`: rates and escape savings now come from the year's tables, so Scottish pre-2024/25 trap rates follow the Higher band (e.g. 63 in 2023/24). The 1.5x taper multiplier is verified for every year from 2020/21 (item 7, ITA 2007 s.35).

(e) UNVERIFIED: Class 2 NI (the 2023/24 small profits threshold is now 6,725, read on the GOV.UK rates and allowances page; Class 2 is not used by any calculation) and Plan 5 thresholds for 2023/24 to 2025/26. The personal allowance taper rate for 2020/21 to 2022/23 is no longer unverified (item 7).

(f) RESOLVED (BE-08 M1, backend audit 2026-11): `getTaxYearForRecordDate` now delegates to `getTaxYearForDate` (built on `getUkTaxYearForDate`, 6 April boundary), so a record dated 3 April 2026 files under 2025/26 and 6 April 2026 under 2026/27. `getRecordValueForMonth` buckets a record dated 1 to 5 April (and a monthly `endDate` of 1 to 5 April) into March of the same calendar year via `getRecordBucketMonth`, so listing, copying and totals still agree. `getAnnualRecordEndDate` follows the same rule (2024-04-01 now ends 2024-04-05); the backend backfills stored `end_date`. Previous behaviour (C3): the whole of April belonged to the new tax year.

(g) RESOLVED (task I8): `calculateDebtSchedule` `interestSavedVsMinimumGbp` now compares against a true minimum-only baseline (each debt pays only its own minimum, no rollover of freed minimums, zero extra budget). Previously the baseline kept the total commitment fixed so freed minimums rolled into the target debt and the saving was understated. Field name unchanged. Because the main plan keeps the commitment fixed, even extra = 0 shows a positive saving for multi-debt portfolios. Not a tax figure.

(h) RESOLVED (task C3): the three apps/web tests were updated (UKTaxCalculator expects 337.5 and 2250 + 337.5; TaxTrapAlertBanner and WealthCheckHeroWidget show effectiveMarginalRatePct, 62; TaxContainer fixtures use 2024-04-06). Original note: three apps/web tests now fail as a consequence of deliberate engine changes and need consumer-track follow-up:

- UKTaxCalculator.test.tsx:61 expects 338 and gets 337.5 (employer NI pence rounding, D5-a).
- TaxTrapAlertBanner.test.tsx:16 expects '62% Marginal Tax'; D7-a gives marginalRatePct 60 (effective 62).
- TaxContainer.test.tsx:313 (D8-a: 1 April now belongs to the previous tax year).

(i) OPEN (task C3 review): `effectiveMarginalRatePct` outside the trap adds uncited constants (8% NI up to 50,270, 2% above, applied for every year and region). Display code no longer uses it outside the trap. Task I6: it now adds the year's table NI rate (class1 main rate to the upper earnings limit, upper rate above, nothing at or below the primary threshold) to the table-derived income tax rate, so the constants are gone; the NI figures for 2022/23 and 2023/24 inherit the approximations in ruling 3.

(j) RESOLVED (task I4I7, I4): `calculateUKEWNITakeHome` charged student loan on pay after pension even without salary sacrifice. Only salary sacrifice now reduces student-loan earnings (as in `calculateUkTax`). Its personal allowance taper is now floored to whole pounds like `calculatePersonalAllowanceForYear`. The 2026/27 fallback for an unsupported year was kept (callers pass `getCurrentUKTaxYear()`, which never throws after the last supported year, so throwing would crash the UI); it is recorded in the code and here, and remains open with (b) and (c).

(k) RESOLVED (task I4I7, I7): `calculateUkTax` now rounds every result-boundary money figure to pence with `roundPence` (grossIncomeGbp, adjustedNetIncomeGbp, taxableIncomeGbp, employeePensionDeductionGbp, employerPensionContributionGbp, employerNiSavingsGbp, giftAidContributionsGbp, totalDeductionsGbp, pensionAllowanceRemainingGbp). Internal arithmetic stays unrounded; net take-home is still computed from the unrounded deductions, so it can differ by a penny from rounded gross minus rounded deductions.

(l) PARTLY RESOLVED (E22 PR 411), deadline 6 April 2027 for the tables: no 2027/28 tables exist and they must still be added (income tax EWNI and Scotland, National Insurance, employer NI, student loans, dividends and savings, `UK_TAX_VECTOR_YEARS` and vectors) before 6 April 2027. What changed: the resolver behaves one way. `getUkTaxYearContext()` is the only place that substitutes a year (after the last supported year it uses the latest supported rates and reports `isFallback: true`, which the tax page shows as a banner). `resolveUkTaxYear()` with no argument and the legacy `getCurrentUKTaxYear()` follow that context and no longer throw or return an unsupported label. Every calculator (`calculateUKTax`, `calculateUKEWNITakeHome`, `calculateUKTakeHome`, the trap helpers) throws `UnsupportedUkTaxYearError` for an EXPLICIT unsupported year; the 2024/25 and 2026/27 silent fallbacks in (b), (c) and (j) are removed. Still open: only the tax page shows the banner; the MCP default year `2024/25` and the baseline year `2025/26` are E17 and E23 items.

(m) RESOLVED (task C3): web default-date regression. D8-a made 1 to 5 April belong to the previous tax year, so the web app's default date for a new tax record, `${startYear}-04-01`, filed new records under the previous year. The default is now `${startYear}-04-06` (apps/web). Covered by unit tests only, not checked in a browser or Storybook. See (f) for the record-date rule.

(n) OPEN: Plan 5 student loan 2023/24 to 2025/26. 25,000 is on the GOV.UK 2026 to 2027 employer page but not on the 2023/24, 2024/25 or 2025/26 pages. Also, the earliest Plan 5 repayment starts the April after the borrower leaves the course, so the three vectors expecting 900 (`studentLoan.<year>.plan5.35000`) are questionable even if the threshold is right. They stay UNVERIFIED. See item 12.
