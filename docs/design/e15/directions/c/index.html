          Rationale and trade-offs

          Fit with principles

          • Every number traceable to inputs: the coach card breaks its claim into three visible facts (APR, interest per month, what £50 changes) rather than one persuasive sentence, and the preview panel in onboarding shows take-home, surplus and net worth together so the link between inputs and outputs is obvious.
          • Show the next action: the coach card is the first thing under the greeting and the only filled primary button in the dashboard viewport. This is the strongest of the three directions on this principle.
          • Calm by default, detail on demand: generous whitespace, soft shadows, plain headings ("Where you stand", "What to do next"). The 24-row schedule sits in a closed drawer. Reassuring micro-copy ("Your figures are safe", "No pressure") lowers anxiety around debt.

          Build cost across 12 screens: M

          Tokens, radii and shadows are a global swap (cheap), and the spacious density needs no new layout primitives. The cost is in craft: a coach card with an illustration slot, about six abstract SVG illustrations for empty and error states, a custom range input, a smoothed chart line with rounded caps in the chart library, and reviewing every page for the larger padding. It is not L because nothing structural changes in KpiStrip, SimulatorSplitLayout or CollapsibleDrawer.

          Mobile fit

          Strong. 48 to 56px targets are already the default, the KPI strip becomes a 2x2 compact grid (chosen over horizontal scroll so all four numbers are visible without gesture discovery), the coach card collapses to a stacked layout with a small illustration, and the mortgage controls fold into an "Adjust" summary bar. Cost: pages are taller than in a dense direction, so users scroll more.

          Risks

          • The soft, friendly tone can feel less authoritative on serious topics such as debt and tax. Copy must stay precise even when it is warm.
          • Lower density hides secondary data. Power users may want a compact mode (a spacing token swap would cover it).
          • Apricot for liabilities sits near the warning status colour. Meaning is carried by wording ("owed") and position, not hue alone, but the two should be reviewed together.
          • Teal brand next to a green "positive" status needs the hue gap kept; a colour-blind simulation pass is recommended.
          • Two web fonts add payload. Falling back to ui-rounded keeps the feel acceptable if they fail to load.
