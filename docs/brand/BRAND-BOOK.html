01

Character and voice

A calm, numerate friend who shows the working, treats every household equally and never judges anyone's money.

Brand promise

Flump: finance without the fog

Three questions govern every screen: can someone worried about money read it without feeling judged, is there one clear thing to look at first, and does it feel like the same product everywhere?

One voice from first visit to daily check-in

The home page opens with a plain statement of what Flump holds, paired with an illustrative monthly picture so an outsider understands the product in seconds.

Desktop (1280px) and phone (375px) views of the home page. Switch Light or Dark at the top to compare Mint and Midnight.

Two moods, one standard

In light mode, Mint pairs a pale mint canvas with deep-green ink and charcoal primary buttons. In dark mode, Midnight steps cool charcoal surfaces lighter toward the viewer, with mint headline figures and warm apricot for borrowing.

The marketing site adds lit glass orbs, paper documents and unhurried scenes. Inside the web and phone apps, surfaces stay quiet so people can read their own numbers without distraction.

Plain

Everyday words, short sentences under 25 words and the figure up front.

Approved copy "Add your first account to see what you own and what you owe."

Factual

States what a number is and what it assumes, never telling a reader what to do.

Approved copy "Estimate: paid off in Mar 2030, if payments stay at £320 a month."

Even-handed

Renting, paying a mortgage, carrying debt or saving all appear in the same calm voice.

Approved copy "You owe £8,420 across 3 debts."

Honest

Labels estimates and sample figures openly and owns mistakes without blaming the person.

Approved copy "We could not save that change. Your figures are unchanged. Try again."

Guidance, not advice, and five no-shaming tests

Flump provides calculations and estimates, never financial recommendations. Any encouragement feature must pass all five tests: no points, scores or financial ranks; no comparison with other people; no guilt-driven streaks or "missed" months; acknowledge the check-in action rather than the amount; and equal warmth for every starting point.

Spacing and responsive steps

A 4 pixel spacing scale keeps cards, gutters and sections aligned from a 375 pixel phone up to a 1440 pixel desktop.

  • Phone navigation switches to a compact top bar at 768 pixels and expands to the full navigation bar at 1024 pixels.
  • Interactive buttons and links preserve a 44 by 44 pixel minimum tap target even in compact density.
  • What-if modelling zones use a dashed border with no background fill so estimates never look like saved balances.

Where illustrations belong

  • Illustrations are decorative and always sit beside plain text carrying the full meaning.
  • Empty states pair one paper picture with one sentence and one clear action.

What we leave out

  • Never draw people, faces, piles of coins, upward-arrow growth graphics, red fills, chequered flags or glass objects inside pictures.

Three motion tiers inside the apps

Loading indicators wait 200 milliseconds before appearing so quick responses never flicker. Once a person's figures are on screen, nothing moves them unexpectedly.

Tier 1

Always fine

Changes no layout, timing or reading order.

  • Neutral glass card surfaces
  • Still document pictures
  • Crisp focus rings and quiet hover lights
Tier 2

Named moments only

Plays once per visit after content is already readable.

  • Chart line drawing itself once
  • Saved figure confirmation highlight
  • Monthly check-in advance and save tick

Tier 3

Never in the app

Delays reading or animates what holds real money.

  • Scroll-pinned scenes, 3D graphics or parallax
  • Floating bubble fields behind numbers
  • Count-up or rolling numbers on real balances

Where figures may move

Example

Real balances never count up, roll or glide inside the apps. On the marketing site only, a fixed sample figure tagged "Example" may count up once on first view, and the interactive pay rise calculator glides smoothly between inputs.

Reduced motion and pause controls

When reduced motion is active, transitions become instant or short fades and ambient scenes stop. The marketing site renders its finished still state in HTML first and shows a visible "Pause animation" and "Play animation" control on scenes lasting over 5 seconds.

Charts

Every chart answers one clear question at a glance, labels lines directly and pairs colour with dashes, markers or text.

Self-drawing chart line

Line charts draw once when first seen, then stay still while you inspect points or drag a scenario slider.

Consistent series colour

Colour identifies an account or category across the product rather than grading gains or losses.

Net worth and mortgage Primary teal line
Savings Sage pot growth
What you owe Warm apricot, never red
Future estimates Dashed line plus range band

Structure and accessibility

  • Every chart pairs a plain takeaway title with a visible one-sentence summary (at most two figures) and a "Show data" table.
  • Use lines for change over time, sorted horizontal bars for parts of a whole, and progress bars against a limit; cap donut charts at five slices including "Other".
  • Label up to three series directly at the end of the line so readers never have to bounce between chart and legend.
  • Tap or keyboard focus selects a data point with a fixed readout, and arrow keys step cleanly from point to point.

Axes, estimates and formatting

  • Future estimates use a dashed line with assumptions stated directly beneath; estimates over one year show a shaded range with low and high pound figures in text.
  • Bar axes always start at zero, with no logarithmic scales or second vertical axis.
  • Keep at most six ticks on the horizontal axis, format years in full ("2030") and use three-letter months ("Sep", never "Sept").
  • All axis, legend and tooltip figures use fixed-width UK currency formatting, and unknown values show as "Not set" rather than zero.
10

Do and do not

A quick side-by-side checklist of the non-negotiable standards across copy, colour, layout and motion.

Do

Facts, plain UK English, neutral cards and apricot for debt.

Do not

Judgements, red debt badges, animated money or mixed scenario totals.

Colours and judgement

Do

Use semantic surface, ink and area colours, keep debt in warm apricot and negative balances in standard ink, and use bright mint only as a fill in light mode.

Do not

Hardcode raw colours, put red or warning badges on ordinary debt, or set body text or links in bright mint on a pale background.

Orb palette discipline

Do

Keep violet and rose on glass orbs, the three-bubble logo, small area markers and assigned chart series.

Do not

Use violet or rose as a full band background, card material or tint, or pair an orb colour with debt, gain or loss.

Voice and copy

Do

Write plain UK English in sentence case, keep sentences short, label estimates clearly and tag sample figures as Example.

Do not

Use em dashes, exclamation marks, ampersands in prose, hype words, judgements or target figures chosen for the reader.

Saved figures and scenarios

Do

Keep headline figures and summary strips driven by saved details, and isolate what-if sliders inside dashed zones labelled Illustration, not saved.

Do not

Mix hypothetical inputs into saved totals, place sliders outside a modelling zone, or offer an Apply button that silently overwrites saved data.

Typography and numbers

Do

Use Plus Jakarta Sans for headings, Inter for body text and fixed-width UK formatting on every financial figure.

Do not

Drop below 12 pixels, use weights above 700, style uppercase eyebrows, or use browser number spinners on money and percentage fields.

Motion across platforms

Do

Keep transitions brief inside the apps, reserve scroll scenes and glass orbs for the marketing site, and honour reduced-motion settings everywhere.

Do not

Count up or roll real balances, put 3D graphics or parallax in the apps, or hide page content until scripts load.

Logo and illustrations

Do

Keep half-width clear space around the three-bubble mark, draw pictures from shared shape lists and keep a real-text Example tag on sample cards.

Do not

Place the logo on bright mint hero panels or photographs, recolour or rotate it, or draw faces, coin piles or upward arrows inside pictures.

How the brand has evolved

Two focused weeks in November 2026 moved Flump from a warm cream ledger to Mint and Midnight, paper documents and the three-bubble mark.

14 to 20 November 2026

The first look: warm cream, deep green and sand

The initial release paired warm cream backgrounds and sand-tinted neutrals with deep teal-green headline panels, Plus Jakarta Sans headings and Inter body text. On 15 November, a round of updates added a soft plum accent, simple flat illustrations, the setup progress ring and the plain-language label "Breathing room".

24 and 25 November 2026

Moving to Midnight and Mint

The owner replaced the cream and deep green look on 24 November 2026 because it sat too close to another company's site, and wanted Flump to have its own distinct character. Mint introduced a pale mint canvas with a solid mint headline card and charcoal primary buttons; Midnight introduced cool charcoal surfaces with mint figures. On 25 November, the light logo mark returned from charcoal to deep teal.

15 to 27 November 2026

Pictures: from flat scenes to paper documents

Early illustrations used simple rounded geometric scenes with a sun and dotted arc. On 27 November 2026, Flump moved to tilted paper documents, bank cards and calendar slips drawn from shared shape data, keeping every sample figure clearly marked with a real-text "Example" tag.

26 and 27 November 2026

Designing the home page

Over two days, thirteen interactive motion prototypes tested how the marketing site should move without overwhelming people. Four key stages trace the path from quiet micro-interactions to the chosen design.

27 and 28 November 2026

The finished home page

On 27 and 28 November 2026, the rebuilt home page and feature pages shipped with assembling document cards, glass orbs and the new three-bubble mark, while leaving full-width solid colour bands behind.

12 to 27 November 2026

The logo: from a single blob to three glass bubbles

Flump began with a single teal sphere mark. When the marketing site introduced lit glass bubbles, three design rounds tested clearer silhouettes that could still read at favicon size before landing on the three-bubble mark on 27 November 2026.
