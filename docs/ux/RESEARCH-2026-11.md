# Evidence base for Flump UX guidelines

The research that holds up best for a UK consumer money product is unglamorous: about half of UK adults struggle with everyday percentages, people avoid looking at bad financial news, and position on a common scale is the only chart encoding lay readers decode reliably. So Flump's interfaces should carry meaning in rounded pounds, labelled ranges and plain titles, never in mental arithmetic or colour alone. Several popular "UX laws" (Hick, Miller's 7±2, Doherty, Tesler) are over-claimed and should not justify rules. Fitts's law, Nielsen's response-time limits, Gestalt grouping and working-memory limits of about four chunks should. GOV.UK's field-tested form and input patterns are the best fit for UK users, with one live dispute over when to validate. WCAG 2.2 AA is the practical UK benchmark: no statute names it for private firms, but it is the evidence that the anticipatory Equality Act duty has been met. On native, Apple and Google set stricter targets (44 pt, 48 dp) and require text to scale. Mature multi-platform systems share tokens and specifications, not component code, and the right pipeline for this monorepo is the existing TypeScript tokens as the single source, a small Bun generator for React Native, and a contract test against Panda's own spec export. DTCG tooling waits until a designer needs Figma interchange.

**How to read the marks.** Each citation carries a confidence mark: **[strong]** (replicated research, binding standard or regulator text), **[moderate]** (single good study, official guidance or a large practitioner body) or **[weak]** (expert opinion, secondary summary or another domain). **[unverified]** means the researcher cited the source from memory, a search snippet or a mirror and did not re-read it in November 2026; check the claim before quoting it externally. **[contested]** means the sources disagree. Legal items also carry **[LAW]**, **[REG]**, **[STD]** or **[PLATFORM]**. None of this is legal advice.

## Four laws survive scrutiny, five are folklore

Nielsen's ten heuristics came from a factor analysis of 249 usability problems. They are an inspection checklist, not validated laws, and they produce false positives unless paired with testing ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)) [moderate, unverified]. **Fitts's law** is among the most replicated results in HCI: hit time rises with distance and falls with target size ([Fitts 1954](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0055392)) [strong, unverified]. **Response-time limits** have been stable for decades: 0.1 s feels instant, flow holds to 1 s, and attention is lost at 10 s, where users need percent done and a way to cancel ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)) [strong]. Gestalt common region overrides proximity and similarity ([Palmer 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(92)90014-S)) [strong, unverified]. Working memory holds about **3 to 5 chunks**, not seven ([Cowan 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/)) [strong, unverified].

The over-claimed set is longer. Hick's law holds for choice-reaction tasks, but searching unfamiliar menus scales closer to linearly ([Hick 1952](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470215208416600); [Landauer and Nachbar 1985](https://doi.org/10.1145/1165385.317470)) [strong for the original task, weak as a UI rule, unverified]. Miller himself hedged about "7±2" ([Miller 1956](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0043158)) [unverified], and NN/g calls the seven-item menu cap a misconception, because menus rely on recognition, not recall ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/short-term-memory-and-web-usability/)) [strong]. Jakob's law is a heuristic argument ([Nielsen 2000](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/end-of-web-design/)) [weak to moderate, unverified]. Tesler's law has no empirical base ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/teslers-law/)) [weak, unverified]. The Doherty 400 ms threshold rests on an unreviewed 1982 IBM report ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/)) [weak, unverified]. The peak-end rule is strong in clinical settings and moderate for UI flows ([Kahneman et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x)) [unverified]. It argues for investing in completion and error recovery rather than decoration. Progressive disclosure helps when the split between first and secondary features is right and hurts when users often need the hidden items ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)) [moderate, unverified].

**Design implications**

- Guideline rationales name a mechanism (target size, response time, working memory), never "Hick's law", "Miller's law", "Tesler's law" or "the Doherty threshold".
- No navigation or menu is capped at seven items on memory grounds.
- Each screen has one visually dominant figure and at most four headline comparators.
- The primary action is the largest target in its action area.

## Half of UK adults cannot do the sums, so the interface must

About **49% of working-age adults in England** had numeracy at the level expected of a primary-school child in 2011 ([National Numeracy](https://www.nationalnumeracy.org.uk/numerate-nation)) [strong, dated]. Later charity surveys put it at 54% to 56% ([National Numeracy](https://nationalnumeracy.org.uk/news/new-survey-uk-numeracy); [KCL](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/policy-institute/assets/national-numeracy-day-2019.pdf)) [moderate]. The FCA's Financial Lives survey found **49% of adults show at least one characteristic of vulnerability** and 24% have low financial resilience ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/more-people-have-bank-accounts-one-ten-have-no-cash-savings)) [strong]. People linearise compound growth, which is linked to borrowing more and saving less ([Stango and Zinman 2009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01483.x)) [strong, unverified]. Natural frequencies improve probabilistic reasoning, though the size of the effect is disputed ([McDowell, Galesic, Gigerenzer 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/medema/v38y2018i3p390-399.html)) [moderate, contested in size].

Avoidance is the emotional pattern to design for. Investors log in less as volatility rises ([Sicherman et al.](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/25997/sicherman_financial_attention.pdf)) [strong], and people avoid useful, free information because it hurts ([Golman, Hagmann, Loewenstein 2017](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:aea:jeclit:v:55:y:2017:i:1:p:96-135)) [strong]. UK brokerage data show a counter "meerkat effect" in some conditions ([City, University of London](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16280/1/Meerkat_July2014_Final.pdf)) [moderate, contested]. The scarcity-bandwidth claim that money worry lowers cognition largely failed replication: 4 of 20 studies held ([Collins summary](https://jasoncollins.blog/posts/replicating-scarcity)) [strong that the effect is unreliable]. Red-shown losses push lab participants toward risk aversion ([Bazley, Cronqvist, Mormann](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2989924)) [moderate, unverified].

On trust, the evidence is thinner than practitioners suggest. Open Banking guidance favours brand names over legal names, strongly branded bank hand-offs and a three-step consent model that states what data is read, why and for how long ([OBL guidelines](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/Customer-Experience-Guidelines-V3.1.3-web.pdf)) [strong]. The FCA's 2026 review praised calculators, walkthroughs and tested summaries, and criticised "cosmetic changes" ([Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/03/fca-publishes-good-practice-and-areas-for-improvement-in-relation-to-consumer-understanding-under-the-consumer-duty/)) [strong]. The FCA endorses layering, with key information up front ([FG22/5](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/finalised-guidance/fg22-5-final-non-handbook-guidance-firms-consumer-duty)) [strong, unverified], and expects comprehension testing with diverse customers. One good-practice firm targeted 80% correct recall ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/consumer-understanding-good-practice-areas-improvement)) [strong].

For habits, defaults beat reminders. Opt-out payroll saving lifted participation by about 50 points over opt-in ([MaPS evidence hub](https://evidence-hub.maps.org.uk/en/research-library/review/workplace-emergency-saving--a-landscape-review-of-existing-evidence)) [moderate, sponsor-reported]. In an FCA experiment, push notifications raised trading by 11% and points and prize draws by 12% ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-keeps-trading-apps-under-review-over-gaming-concerns)) [strong]. Gamified features were linked to riskier trading ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/research-articles/gaming-trading-how-trading-apps-could-be-engaging-consumers-worse)) [strong]. Fresh-start landmarks (new month, birthday) prompt goal pursuit ([Dai, Milkman, Riis 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)) [moderate, non-finance], and time preferences shift around payday (Carvalho et al., via [Collins](https://jasoncollins.blog/posts/replicating-scarcity)) [moderate, secondary]. Published onboarding drop-off figures for finance apps are scarce. Open Banking says app-to-app authentication raises consent success and loads should stay under 3 s ([OBL](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/insights/improving-customer-journeys/)) [moderate, figures unpublished].
**Design implications**

- Projections show rounded pounds with a low, central and high range, in today's money for horizons over a year, with assumptions one tap away from a single shared assumptions source.
- Rates and APRs always appear alongside a pound equivalent; comparisons show the difference directly.
- Danger red is reserved for states needing action now; debt balances and negative net worth are not red by default, and colour is never the only cue.
- Any caveat that changes a headline's meaning sits in layer 1 or 2, never only in a drawer.
- Every calculated figure has a "how this was worked out" disclosure with inputs and data freshness; user-entered and bank-synced figures are visually distinct.
- No streaks, points, confetti or leaderboards on financial actions; notifications go out only for things the user asked for or that need action.
- The default check-in cadence is monthly, anchored to payday or the start of the month, and user-adjustable.
- Calculators work without an account; manual entry is a first-class alternative to bank linking; sample data is unmistakably labelled.
- Protective settings are easy on and slower off; leaving, unlinking, exporting and deleting are as easy as joining.
- Usability tests include low-numeracy participants and a restate-the-meaning comprehension check with a target.

## Charts must work for the third with low graph literacy

Position on a common scale beats length, angle and area, a ranking replicated with crowdsourced participants ([Heer and Bostock 2010](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jheer/files/2010-MTurk-CHI.pdf)) [strong]. Pies are not mainly read by angle, and donuts match pies for accuracy ([Skau and Kosara 2016](https://www.tableau.com/research/publications/arcs-angles-or-areas-individual-data-encodings-pie-and-donut-charts)) [moderate]. **About a third of adults** have both low graph literacy and low numeracy ([Galesic and Garcia-Retamero 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/medema/v31y2011i3p444-457.html)) [strong]. Recall follows the chart title more than the data, even when they conflict ([Kong, Liu, Karahalios 2019](https://mail.zcliu.cs.umd.edu/vistitles/degree-of-misalignment-CHI19.pdf)) [moderate]. The UK Government Analysis Function sets practical caps: pies only with five or fewer parts, four lines per chart, four categories per stack, bars ranked and zero-based, direct labels instead of legends, no dual axes, and a text alternative or table for every chart ([Analysis Function](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)) [moderate].

Uncertainty is better shown than hidden. Numeric ranges barely reduce trust, while verbal caveats reduce it more ([van der Bles et al. 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/305110478.pdf)) [strong]. A 2026 Bank of England paper with about 1,600 UK participants found fan charts well understood and best at conveying expectation and uncertainty together ([BoE](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2026/anchors-aweigh-the-effect-of-communicating-forecast-uncertainty)) [moderate]. Quantile dot plots improved estimates on mobile ([Kay et al. 2016](https://vis.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Kay-WhenishIsMyBus.pdf)) [strong], but a Dutch pension study found dot plots "complex" ([Netspar](https://www.netspar.nl/en/knowledge-hub/in-conversation-about-visualizations-of-expected-pension-amount/)) [weak for UK]. Lay readers treat cones as the thing itself ([Joslyn and Savelli](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2020.590232/pdf)) [moderate]. Truncated y-axes inflate perceived effects even when viewers know about the truncation ([Correll, Bertini, Franconeri 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02035v2)) [strong]. Log scales worsened lay understanding in one study, though a Canadian study found no effect ([LSE](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/106217)) [moderate, contested]. On phones, finger occlusion and fat-finger errors break hover tooltips ([Snyder et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11602)) [moderate]. No study sets a maximum series count for phones; "one to three" is inference [weak].

**Design implications**

- Category breakdowns over five parts use sorted horizontal bars with £ labels; pies and donuts are allowed only at five or fewer parts that sum to a whole.
- Every chart has a takeaway title, a factual subtitle, direct labels, a visible one-sentence summary and a "show as table" route.
- Projections beyond a year show a central line with graded bands and numeric £ edges, never a lone deterministic line.
- Bar axes start at zero; a zero line is drawn whenever values cross zero; no log scales or dual y-axes in consumer views.
- Charts have at most four series on desktop and three on phones.
- Touch charts use tap-to-select with a fixed readout panel, and keyboard arrows step through points with announcements.

## GOV.UK patterns fit UK forms, with one live dispute

GOV.UK guidance asks for visible labels above fields, units in the label, "(optional)" rather than asterisks, an error summary that takes focus, inline messages that match it, and inputs that are never cleared ([Text input](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/text-input/); [Error message](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/)) [strong]. "One thing per page" suits onboarding and imports ([Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/)) [strong], but there is no evidence it transfers to repeat-use dashboards. **Validation timing is contested.** GOV.UK validates on submit, not on blur ([Validation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)) [strong as a standard]. Wroblewski's 22-person study found on-blur validation gave 22% more success and 42% faster completion, concentrated on hard fields, with validate-on-focus worst ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/inline-validation-in-web-forms/)) [moderate]. Baymard favours inline validation that clears errors once they are fixed ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)) [unverified].

For money, avoid `type="number"`: scrolling changes values and invalid input is dropped silently ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/text-input/)) [strong]. Use text with `inputmode`, a £ prefix outside the field, "in pounds" in the label, and parsing that ignores punctuation ([Validation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)) [strong]. Accepting thousands separators is inferred from that rule, not stated in it. Some keypads lack a minus sign, so negative-capable fields use plain text. Dates use three fields, accept month names and never auto-tab ([Date input](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/date-input/)) [strong]. Sliders are imprecise and exclude some motor-impaired users ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gui-slider-controls/)) [moderate]. Users who fail with a slider fall back to text fields ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/slider-interfaces)) [moderate, unverified]. Defaults act as anchors ([Tversky and Kahneman 1974](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124); [Madrian and Shea 2001](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355301753265543)) [strong, unverified].

For loading and feedback, show no indicator under 1 s, a spinner for single-module waits of 2 to 10 s, skeletons for full pages, and a progress bar with an estimate beyond 10 s ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)) [weak to moderate]. Confirmations work only if they are rare, specific and use outcome-labelled buttons; undo is preferred ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)) [moderate]. Empty states should explain the area and offer the action that populates it ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)) [moderate, unverified]. On navigation, hidden desktop navigation was used in 27% of cases against 48 to 50% for visible navigation, and on mobile four or fewer items should stay visible ([NN/g, 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)) [moderate to strong, dated]. For motion, about 100 ms suits feedback and 200 to 300 ms transitions, with exits shorter than entrances ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)) [weak to moderate]. Vestibular harm from motion is well documented ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)) [strong].

**Design implications**

- Validation runs on submit and on the server. On-blur validation is allowed only for fields with strict, non-obvious rules, never on focus or per keystroke, and errors clear once fixed.
- Live calculators flag or clamp out-of-range input beside the field and never clear it (inference).
- Every money field is `type="text" inputmode="decimal"` with an external £ prefix, "in pounds" in its accessible name, tolerant parsing and en-GB formatting applied on blur.
- Every slider has an editable value; skewed ranges use non-linear scales; calculator defaults come from saved data or a sourced, labelled assumption, never an optimistic return.
- Recalculation lands under 100 ms; operations over 10 s show progress, an estimate and cancel.
- Common deletions offer undo; irreversible ones use a dialog that names the item, with outcome-labelled buttons and no default.
- Motion tokens are 100 ms (feedback), 200 ms (exit) and 250 to 300 ms (enter), with no money count-ups under reduced motion.

## WCAG 2.2 AA is the benchmark, the Equality Act is the law

WCAG 2.2 (12 December 2024) adds six A or AA criteria: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured, 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, 2.5.8 Target Size of 24 by 24 CSS px, 3.2.6 Consistent Help, 3.3.7 Redundant Entry and 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication. It also removes 4.1.1 Parsing ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)) [STD, strong]. For finance, the criteria that bite hardest are 2.5.7 (sliders need a non-drag route), 1.4.11 (3:1 for chart marks and control boundaries), 3.3.4 (review or undo before changing saved data) and 4.1.3 (announce recalculated outcomes without moving focus). The APG slider pattern warns that touch screen readers may not send key events ([APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/)) [strong]. WCAG 2.2 became ISO/IEC 40500:2025 ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/news/2025/web-content-accessibility-guidelines-wcag-2-2-approved-as-iso-iec-international-standard)) [STD]. EN 301 549 V4.1.1 moved to WCAG 2.2 in September 2026 ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/fr/blog/en-301-549-v4-1-1-is-final-what-changed-what-it-means-and-what-you-should-do/)), but whether it yet carries presumption of conformity is **[contested]** ([BrowserStack](https://www.browserstack.com/blog/what-en-301-549-v4-1-1-means-for-your-accessibility-strategy/)).

The Equality Act 2010 s.20 duty covers providing information "in an accessible format" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/20)) [LAW]. It is anticipatory for services ([EHRC Code](https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/guidance/codes-practice/code-practice-services-public-functions-and-associations/chapter-seven)) [LAW, unverified, snippet only], and a revised Code is pending. That the Act covers websites is the common reading, not statutory text. The FCA links Equality Act breaches to fair treatment ([FG21/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/finalised-guidance/guidance-firms-fair-treatment-vulnerable-customers)) [REG]. Whether the Consumer Duty applies directly depends on Flump's unknown authorisation status, which needs a legal check. The European Accessibility Act is unlikely to apply unless Flump serves EU consumers ([Macfarlanes](https://www.macfarlanes.com/insights/102kb3l/european-accessibility-act-application-to-firms)) [LAW, EU; reading uncertain].

On native, WCAG2ICT (December 2025) reads "web page" as "software program" ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/)) [STD, informative]. Android requires 48 by 48 dp targets ([Android](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)) [PLATFORM, strong]. React Native exposes `accessibilityRole="adjustable"`, `accessibilityValue` text, live regions and `isReduceMotionEnabled` ([RN](https://reactnative.dev/docs/accessibility); [AccessibilityInfo](https://reactnative.dev/docs/accessibilityinfo)) [PLATFORM]. Automated tools find a minority of barriers: 57% by volume according to Deque ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/automated-accessibility-testing-coverage/)) and at best 41% per tool in the GDS 2017 test ([GDS](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2017/02/24/what-we-found-when-we-tested-tools-on-the-worlds-least-accessible-webpage/)) [moderate]. GOV.UK sets the minimum assistive technology combinations to test ([Service Manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/technology/testing-with-assistive-technologies)) [GP]. Under forced colours, browsers strip shadows and background images ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors)) [STD].

**Design implications**

- Every drag interaction (slider, chart brush, reorder, swipe-dismiss) has a single-tap alternative.
- Sticky headers, banners, bottom sheets and CTA bars never fully cover a focused element.
- Every semantic token pair passes 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for non-text in both themes, including chart series against `surface.card`.
- Recalculated outcomes and toasts use `role="status"` (web) or `announceForAccessibility` and live regions (native); undo toasts stay until dismissed or pause on hover and focus.
- Wizards never ask for the same data twice; help sits in the same place on every page and step; password and OTP fields allow paste and password managers.
- Commits that change saved data have a review step or undo.
- State is shown with real borders or outlines, not shadows, so it survives forced colours.
- Native text never disables font scaling on body or figures, and layouts reflow at AX5 and Android 200%.
- Each release gets a manual pass: keyboard, 400% zoom, text spacing, forced colours, NVDA with Chrome, VoiceOver iOS and TalkBack.

## Platforms agree on structure and differ on chrome

Apple's figures are a 44 by 44 pt default target (28 pt minimum), 17 pt body text (11 pt minimum), text enlargeable to at least 200%, and contrast checked in both appearances ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)) [PLATFORM, strong]. Custom fonts must match the accessibility behaviour of system fonts ([HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)) [strong]. Liquid Glass is for controls and navigation only, never content ([HIG Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)) [strong]. iOS 27 reportedly removes the opt-out ([Design for Native](https://designfornative.com/what-designers-need-to-know-about-ios-27/)) [weak, press]. Tab bars are for navigation, not actions, and Apple says to avoid the More overflow tab ([HIG Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)) [strong]. Destructive choices go in action sheets ([HIG Action sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-sheets)) [strong]. On Android 16, edge-to-edge cannot be opted out of and predictive back is on by default ([Android 16](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)) [strong, via snippet]. Window size classes break at 600, 840, 1200 and 1600 dp ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes)) [strong]. Material's 48 dp targets, type scale, motion tokens and dynamic colour come from pages that could not be rendered ([M3](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration/tokens-specs)) [unverified]. Third-party sources disagree on which curve "emphasized" maps to [contested]. Revolut implements one design system natively per platform ([Revolut](https://medium.com/revolut/the-fundamentals-of-ios-at-revolut-f75acb765ac8)) [moderate]. The App Store requires in-app account deletion and value beyond a "repackaged website" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) [strong], and Google Play requires the same deletion path ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)) [strong, via snippet].

**Design implications**

- These stay identical across platforms: information architecture (same tabs, names and order), semantic colour tokens, brand typeface (scaled), chart styling, number formatting, and empty and error states.
- These differ on purpose: back gesture, sheet and dialog chrome and button order, title alignment, haptics, native date pickers, switch styling, navigation material, and large-screen navigation (sidebar on iPad, rail on Android).
- Native touch targets are at least 48 units; web uses 44 px under `pointer: coarse` and never goes below 24 px.
- Brand colour lives in content (cards, hero, charts), never in glass bars or behind financial figures; wallpaper dynamic colour never drives status or chart colours.
- The Expo app uses native stacks for back behaviour, handles safe-area insets, and offers native value beyond the web app.
- Web breakpoints align with 600, 840 and 1200; web never imitates edge-swipe back, glass chrome or action sheets.

## Mature systems share tokens and specifications, not components

Microsoft Fluent stores tokens "in a universal format very similar to CSS" and converts them per platform, with platform overrides under a `platform` key ([Fluent pipeline](https://microsoft.github.io/fluentui-token-pipeline/json.html)) [moderate]. Expo DOM components run web React in a webview with "longer loading times", which suits occasional chart-heavy screens rather than core flows ([Expo](https://expo.dev/blog/the-magic-of-expo-dom-components)) [moderate]. DTCG 2025.10 is the first stable token format, but it is a Community Group report, not a W3C standard. Its Resolver module expresses themes and modes ([W3C CG](https://www.w3.org/community/reports/design-tokens/CG-FINAL-resolver-20251028/)) [strong]. Terrazzo 2.10 supports resolvers ([Terrazzo](https://terrazzo.app/docs/guides/resolvers/)) [moderate]. Style Dictionary 5.8 handles DTCG value shapes but has no modes and no React Native format ([SD changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/style-dictionary/style-dictionary/main/CHANGELOG.md)) [moderate]. Panda 2.5 can export its resolved system, including per-condition dark values, with `panda codegen --spec` ([Panda](https://panda-css.com/docs/theming/spec)) [moderate]. Whether this survived the move to v2 is **[contested]** (a snippet said "not ported"), so run `panda codegen --help` to confirm. Panda's config is DTCG-like but not DTCG JSON ([Panda tokens](https://panda-css.com/docs/theming/tokens)). Unistyles 3.8.1 takes a plain theme object with adaptive light and dark themes ([Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/unistyles-3-0-beyond-react-native-stylesheet)) [moderate]. NativeWind 5 is still a release candidate ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/nativewind)). Hermes uses each platform's native Intl, so en-GB currency output can differ between iOS and Android ([Hermes](https://github.com/facebook/hermes/blob/main/doc/IntlAPIs.md)) [moderate].
**Design implications**

- Tokens and component specifications (variant names and allowed values) are shared; component implementations and `cva`/`sva` recipes are not.
- No token name encodes a mode (no `surface.card.dark`).
- One formatting package serves web and native, with snapshot tests on both platforms.
- A parity matrix (component by platform, status, story link) is kept as data and checked by a test.

### Recommended token pipeline

**Source.** `packages/ui/src/tokens/*.ts` remains the only authored source, in the six semantic namespaces with light and dark as Panda conditions. Panda consumes it directly for web and marketing.

**Generate.** A Turborepo `generate:tokens` task runs a Bun script, roughly 150 lines with no new dependencies, that walks the same TS object and writes `packages/tokens-native/theme.ts`. That file holds typed light and dark objects with identical semantic paths, unitless numbers, font weights as strings, and shadows as RN `boxShadow` or elevation. Unistyles 3 (or plain `StyleSheet`) consumes it. The file is committed and never edited by hand.

**Check.** CI regenerates the theme and fails on any diff. A Vitest contract test reads `panda codegen --spec` output (`styled-system/specs/design-system.json`) and `theme.ts`, and asserts that every semantic path exists in both with equal resolved values in both themes. `scripts/check-design-tokens.ts` is extended to scan `apps/native/**` for colour literals. Storybook for React Native stories reuse the web story names. Committed diffs of generated files act as the token changelog.

**Later.** If a designer adopts Figma Variables, emit DTCG 2025.10 with a Resolver file from the same TS source and process it with Terrazzo rather than Style Dictionary, because of resolver support. DTCG never becomes the authored source.

## Conclusion

Flump's main risk is not that users find it ugly but that they misread a figure, avoid a bad number, or get nudged into activity they did not choose. Comprehension testing with low-numeracy users and restraint in engagement mechanics are worth more than polish. Three gaps need Flump's own research: pension range formats with UK savers, red versus neutral debt colouring, and check-in cadence. For parity, treat the TypeScript tokens and component specifications as the product and each platform's components as renderings of them, so chrome can differ deliberately while meaning, numbers and colour semantics stay identical.
