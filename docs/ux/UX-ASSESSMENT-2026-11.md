# UX assessment, November 2026

Date: 2026-11-14. Assessed: the web app and marketing site on `origin/main` (commit `79b9bc9d`), against [UX-GUIDELINES.md](./UX-GUIDELINES.md), [STYLE-GUIDE.md](./STYLE-GUIDE.md), [CROSS-PLATFORM.md](./CROSS-PLATFORM.md) and the research in [RESEARCH-2026-11.md](./RESEARCH-2026-11.md).

This is a dated assessment, not a rule set. Its findings become rules only where they have entered the three documents above.

## 1. Verdict

The foundations are better than the screens. Flump has a tested token system, a real component library, a firm rule separating saved facts from what-ifs, a no-judgement rule, and machine checks for claims and advice wording. Few products of this size have any of those. What users see today does not yet show it: pages carry three to ten times the figures a person can take in, the answer to the page's question is often not the headline, notices are louder than content, and light mode is flat.

The polish plan (PRs 1 to 13) is the right response to most of that, and the research supports its main rules. It leaves six gaps, all about whether people can _use and trust_ the figures rather than how the screens look: money entry, honest forecasts, figures that explain themselves, page-level accessibility, visible save feedback, and evidence that real users understand the result. Those are section 4 and the extra PR list in section 7.

## 8. Notes for the polish plan

The plan needs no structural change. Three small reconciliations, for PR 13 or whoever next edits it:

1. **Rationale labels.** P1 cites "Miller's law" and P5 "Hick's law". The rules are sound; the labels are not. The research supports a working-memory limit of about four items (P1.1 already uses four) and finds Hick's law weak as an interface rule. UX-GUIDELINES states the mechanisms instead.
2. **Charts.** P10 should gain the range on long forecasts (subject to U1), a 5-part cap for donuts, and tap or keyboard access to values. These are Extra B.
